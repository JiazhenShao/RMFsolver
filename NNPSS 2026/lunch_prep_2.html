Your work in three sentences (the hook)
"I calculate how fast the nuclear–quark boundary can actually move in a merger. Deconfinement is instant, but the conversion is rate-limited by the weak process d u ↔ u s making strangeness, plus strangeness diffusing back to the front — so we solve a steady-state deflagration front. We find it creeps at only hundreds of m/s at merger temperatures, roughly 10⁴ times slower than the instant conversion that current merger simulations assume."

That last clause is the memorable part: your result says a standard modeling assumption is questionable. That's what makes someone lean in.

The one physics ask worth having ready

This is the question that sits exactly on the overlap between your calculation and his expertise — the best possible thing to ask him specifically.

Ask
"On the quark side we treat it as unpaired massless u/d/s. If that matter is actually color-superconducting, would the gap change the weak rate or the strangeness diffusion enough to matter for the boundary speed?"

Why it lands: it shows you understand both sides, it's specific, and it's the kind of question that turns into "you should look at…" or a real back-and-forth.

Knowing "enough" color superconductivity

You need vocabulary and landscape, not depth — framed around what's relevant to your front.

  • 1Quark Cooper pairing ≈ BCS, but color + flavor structure replaces simple spin singlets.

Thread A — what it is

In a rotating color superconductor, quantized non-Abelian semi-superfluid vortices form. Their cores trap Nambu-Goldstone modes and Majorana fermion zero modes — the same object CM physicists chase in topological superconductors.
