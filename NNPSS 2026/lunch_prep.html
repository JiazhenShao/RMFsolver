Your work in three sentences (the hook)
"I calculate how fast the nuclear–quark boundary can actually move in a merger. Deconfinement is instant, but conversion is rate-limited by the weak process d u ↔ u s making strangeness, plus strangeness diffusing back to the front. The punchline does double duty: it questions an assumption in post-merger simulations, and it justifies an assumption in the inspiral interface-mode papers. Both are conversation openers.

Already visited and showed interest in how the transition affects neutrino signals. r-process + neutrino physics in mergers and supernovae. The chain: phase transition → altered thermodynamics & neutrino emission → ejecta electron fraction Ye → r-process yields & kilonova.

The smoking gun: the QCD "second neutrino burst"

In a core-collapse supernova, a first-order transition causes a second collapse and second shock, seen as a second neutrino burst. Its fingerprint is the flavor: unlike the electron-neutrino first burst, the second is dominated by electron antineutrinos (degeneracy is lifted as the second shock crosses neutronized matter).

Sagert et al. 2009 (PRL) — the signal Dasgupta et al. 2010 — detectability Fischer / Bastian — modern EoS, onset density FFC — fast flavor conversion in the burst
Why it connects to your work

These predictions assume the transition proceeds promptly once conditions are met. Your finite-conversion-speed result bears directly on whether the second burst is sharp, smeared, or delayed. And your front is driven by the same weak processes that emit neutrinos — your draft currently defers Urca/neutrinos, which is the natural extension that speaks straight to this community.

Thread A — what it is

In a rotating color superconductor, non-Abelian semi-superfluid vortices form. Their cores trap Nambu-Goldstone modes and Majorana fermion zero modes — the same object CM physicists chase in topological superconductors.
